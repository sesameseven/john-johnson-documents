The cost of the complete scheme, i.e., the scheme for obtaining 4,000,000 gallons
of water daily, is estimated by Mr. Mansergh at £430,000, but as it is proposed
at the outset to lay mains to carry but 2,500,000 daily, the initial outlay is
estimated at £330,000. When the requirements of the District justify it the
further sum of £100,000 would be expended in laying an additional main for the
increased supply. It should, however, be clearly understood, that, for at least
two good reasons, if the Bill is promoted it must be for power to carry out the
whole scheme. In the first place it is unlikely, and it would be unreasonable to
expect, that Parliament would allow any Water Authority to appropriate only a
portion of the yield of a water-shed, as it would be impossible for any other
District to make use of that which would be left. In the second place it would,
in the course of time, be necessary to promote a second Bill in Parliament to
procure the extra supply of water and to expend the additional £100,000. The
disadvantages of such a mode of procedure need not be enlarged upon.

It should be mentioned, that, by some, the waters of the Font are looked upon
with some amount of suspicion as being unfit for a town’s supply. The waters are
drained from a peaty district and are somewhat discoloured, but this
discolouration would be largely, if not entirely, removed by storage and
filtration. This aspect of the case has been fully considered by the Medical
Officer for the Borough, and his report upon it, which is appended hereto, will
probably remove all doubts as to the fitness of these waters for a town’s supply.

It is well known that the waters of the Tosson Springs and the River Alwin are
very wholesome and quite free from the objections which may be raised against
those of the Font, and it has been an oft-expressed wish of several prominent
townsmen that a supply for Tynemouth be obtained from the Tosson or the Alwin,
or from both sources.

The exact yield of the Tosson Springs is not known, but it is certain that it
falls very far short of what is required for Tynemouth, and at best it can form
but a supplementary supply. In his report Mr. Mansergh deals with this, and says
“the works on the Font would be at such a level that the Tosson Spring waters
could flow by gravitation into the principal mains, when the time arrives to
secure more water than the Font will yield.”

The River Alwin is at least fifteen miles further from the Tynemouth District
than is the Font, when measured over the route upon which the main would be
laid, and when it is considered that the Alwin is twelve miles from a railway,
whereas the works upon the Font would be in close proximity to one, it will be
seen that the additional cost of carrying out such a scheme, upon that estimated
for the Font, renders it one of such magnitude that it would be financially
impossible for this Borough to undertake it. Besides, as compared with the Font,
there are much larger proprietary interests upon the Alwin which would be

scheme. Further, if such a scheme were promoted it is certain that it would be
