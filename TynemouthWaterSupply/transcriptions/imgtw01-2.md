TYNEMOUTH WATER SUPPLY.

Borough Surveyor’s Office,
Tynemouth,
1st November, 1897.

In the year 1786 the Company of Proprietors of the North Shields Waterworks
was established by Act of Parliament, for the purpose of supplying the
inhabitants of North Shields and Tynemouth with water.

Between the years 1865 and 1870 the supply of water was very inadequate,
and in 1869 Sir John Hawkseley, in a report to the Local Board, stated that
the supply was only from 10 to 14 gallons per day per head of the population.

The supply of water continued to be very unsatisfactory, and various schemes
were proposed for obtaining water from the springs at Tynemouth, from the
Whitley Dean, and from the springs at Cullercoats, but none of these schemes
were carried out.

The Company neglected to apply to Parliament for powers to obtain water
from other sources, and the supply continued to be very insufficient.
