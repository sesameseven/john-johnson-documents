After the Bill had passed the Lords’ Committee, the Town Council appointed a
Sub-committee to redeem the promise made to the Lords, and to consider the
question of improving the water supply of the District. The Sub-committee
entered into negotiations with the Newcastle Company, and also instructed Mr.
James Mansergh, Waterworks Engineer, Westminster, to prepare a scheme for
supplying water to the Tynemouth District from some suitable source. Mr.
Mansergh’s report was presented to the Council in March last, and it is upon
this report and the proposals of the Newcastle Company that the Sub-committee
have based their recommendations.

Mr. Mansergh, in his report, deals with the question of obtaining a supply of
water from the River Alwin, the Barrow Burn, the River Usway, the Tosson
Springs, the River Coquet, and the River Font, and he comes to the conclusion
that the River Font is the most suitable source from which a supply can be
obtained. The River Font rises in the Cheviot Hills, and flows into the River
Coquet at a point about two miles above Rothbury. The watershed of the Font
contains an area of about 17,000 acres, and the average annual rainfall upon it
is about 40 inches. The scheme proposed by Mr. Mansergh is to construct a
reservoir upon the Font, with a capacity of about 790 million gallons, and to
lay filter beds and works in connection therewith. The water would be delivered
by gravitation over a distance of about 25 miles to a service reservoir near
Moorhouses, from which the supply to the District would be distributed.
