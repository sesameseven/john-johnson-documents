years ago Sheffield suffered considerably from lead poisoning due to the lead dissolved in the water.
It was a peaty moorland water, and was distributed unfiltered to the consumers.
To obviate this danger the water was filtered, and a very small quantity of chalk was added to it at an infinitesimal cost. These measures were eminently successful, and since they were adopted the water has had no action whatever on lead. Wakefield, Barnsley, and Keighley have similar supplies and have had
similar experiences. The unfiltered water caused lead poisoning; the water now that is filtered and
limed does not do so. On the other hand, Oldham still distributes the water in its natural state,
and is still subject to lead poisoning.

With these facts before me I am of opinion that the Font water, provided it is filtered and
limed, is suitable for a town supply.

As this opinion is apparently at variance with that expressed by Messrs. Pattinson about one of
the samples, it must be noted that the samples were all submitted to them in their natural state, and
therefore not in the improved condition in which they would be distributed. The protective deposit
mentioned by Mr. Pattinson as forming in water pipes is undoubted, but, of course, some time will
elapse before it forms in new ones.

The gathering ground and sources of the Font were described in my report to the Council in
Committee, dated 23rd August, 1887. The conditions remain unaltered, and it will be enough to
state that, apart from the peaty surface, the gathering ground of the Font is a very favourable one;
the number of inhabitants and cattle is very small. There is is therefore little danger of pollution by
excrement or other animal refuse, and the small quantity of such refuse will be easy dealt with.

In conclusion, I summarise the opinions previously expressed.

The Font water is not suitable for a town supply in its natural state. Settling, continuously
efficient filtration, and liming would greatly improve it, check its lead solvent powers, and render it
suitable. If some discolouration should remain, the objection would be less on health than on
sentimental grounds.

It may be noted that when the first draft of this report was laid before you in August, I was.
still making inquiries as to the experience of other towns whose supplies are similar to that of the
Font, and especially as to the action of such waters on lead. The additional information acquired
necessitated considerable modificatians of the provisional opinions then expressed, and in my opinion
it fully justifies the belief that, subject to proper treatment, the water of the Font is suitable for a
town supply.
