In conclusion, it has transpired within the past few days, that the Newcastle
Company is going to Parliament also to obtain powers to draw a supply of
water from the Font. Here, therefore, is another weighty reason for
Tynemouth pushing its claim for a supply of water from this source.
Tynemouth, while proceeding with its Bill, can still negotiate with the
Newcastle Company respecting the waters from the Font also, but if the
Corporation should not appear before Parliament in the coming session,
and the Newcastle Company should succeed with its Bill, Tynemouth would
be shut out from the Font watershed as it has been from that of the Tyne,
and be left to make the best bargain it can with the Newcastle Company for
a supply of water. If Newcastle should not succeed with its Bill, then
Tynemouth would have nothing to depend upon but the uncertain supply which
it would get from the Newcastle Company's present sources, and for which
it would have to pay at a rate which, in all probability, would result in
a heavy annual loss to the Ratepayers for many years to come.

### Extract from Medical Officer's Report.
TYNEMOUTH,
October 29th, 1897.

---

RIVER FONT. In the following table the most important features of the
three analyses are placed in parallel columns for the purpose of comparison,
and they are arranged, not in order of time, but according to the state of
the weather when the samples were taken.

| Measurement                  |        During a long drought |  After 16 days dry weather  | At close of a wet period |
| -----------                  |    -----------               |  ---------------            | ------------------------ | 
| Total solids in solution     |       14.500 grs.            | 12.940 grs.                 | 9.460 grs. |
| Chlorine                     |       0.850 ,,               | 0.910 ,,                   | 0.910 ,, |
| Ammonia                      |       None.                  | 0.002 ,,                   | 0.001 ,, |
| Albuminoid Ammonia           |       0.006 ,,               | 0.010 ,,                   | 0.010 ,, |
| Nitrogen as Nitrates         |       0.003 ,,               | 0.018 ,,                   | 0.010 ,, |
| Oxygen absorbed in 15 minutes |       0.022 ,,              |  0.111 ,,                  | 0.186 ,, |
| Do. 4 hours                   |      0.131 ,,               | 0.212 ,,                   | 0.312 ,, |
| Hardness before boiling       |      11.9 deg.              | 9.3 deg.                   | 7.0 deg. |
| Do. after do.                 |      3.0 ,,                 | 3.7 ,,                     | 3.6 ,, |
| Appearance in 2 foot tube     |      yellowish colour       | yellowish colour           | marked brownish colour |
| Smell when heated             |      slight fusty           | none                       | slight earthy |

It will be seen at once that the total hardness and the solids in solution
were greatest in dry weather; but the permanent and most objectionable form
of hardness—that which remains after boiling—scarcely varied, and in every
case was small. They may fairly be classed as soft waters, and on that
point no objection can be raised to them.
