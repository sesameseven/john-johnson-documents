# 1. Borough Surveyor’s Report (Page 521)

Municipal Borough of Tynemouth  
Borough Surveyor’s Office  
1 November 1897  

The Borough Surveyor reports on the history and present condition of the water supply to the Borough of Tynemouth. He notes that the supply has historically been obtained from the North Shields Waterworks Company, whose works were originally constructed to supply the town of North Shields. The Company’s works have been repeatedly enlarged, but the supply has remained inadequate for the growing population.

The Surveyor describes the sources of supply, including the Font, a spring situated near the village of Earsdon. He notes that the Font water is soft and of good quality, but the quantity available is insufficient for the needs of the district. He also reports that the Royal Commission on Water Supply had expressed concern about the Font, noting that the water was liable to contamination owing to the geological formation of the district.

The Surveyor concludes that the present supply is insufficient both in quantity and quality, and that additional sources must be obtained.

# 2. Medical Officer’s Report (Page 522)

J. Edward Gofton  
Medical Officer of Health  
1 November 1897  

Dr. Gofton reports on the chemical and sanitary condition of the water supplied to the Borough. He notes that the water from the Font is derived from the Coal Measures, and that such waters are often peaty and liable to dissolve lead from service pipes.

He describes the action of peaty waters on lead, noting that the solvent action is increased when the water is soft and deficient in mineral salts. He cites examples from Sheffield, Wakefield, Barnsley, Keighley, and Oldham, where similar waters have caused cases of lead poisoning.

Dr. Gofton explains that filtration and liming can reduce the solvent action of such waters, and that the North Shields Waterworks Company has adopted these methods. He reports that the treated water is now less liable to dissolve lead, but that the Font water, when untreated, is still dangerous.

He concludes that the Font water should not be used for domestic purposes unless it has been properly treated.

## 📝 Editorial Note

These two reports, presented together, illustrate the dual concerns of the Borough in 1897:  
- **Quantity** — the supply was insufficient for a growing population.  
- **Quality** — untreated Font water posed a risk of lead contamination.

This combined edition preserves the full text of both reports for reference and research.
