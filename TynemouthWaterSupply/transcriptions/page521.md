# Tynemouth Water Supply  
**Borough Surveyor’s Office, Tynemouth**  
**1st November, 1897**

The water supply to Tynemouth and North Shields was first undertaken in 1786 by private individuals, who formed themselves into a company under the title of **“The Company of Proprietors of the North Shields Waterworks.”**

Between the years 1865 and 1870 the Company were unable to meet the demands made upon them for water. In 1869 Sir John Hawkseley, in a report to the Company, stated that the supply was only **10 to 14 gallons per day per head of the population**, and that the supply was quite inadequate to meet the requirements of the district.

From that time until the present the supply has been a source of dissatisfaction to the inhabitants of the district. The Company have made no attempt to improve their sources of supply, and several schemes which have been brought forward have been abandoned.

Tynemouth is situated on the north bank of the Tyne, and the district is supplied with water from the Font. In 1869 the Royal Commission on Water Supply reported that the Font was **not a suitable source of supply** for the district.
