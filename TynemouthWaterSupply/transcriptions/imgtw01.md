TYNEMOUTH WATER SUPPLY

MUNICIPAL BOROUGH OF TYNEMOUTH.

BOROUGH SURVEYOR’S OFFICE,
TYNEMOUTH,
1st November, 1897.

The water supply to Tynemouth and North Shields was first undertaken in 1786 by private individuals, who formed themselves into a company under the title of “The Company of Proprietors of the North Shields Waterworks.” The Company were empowered by an Act of Parliament to supply water to North Shields and the neighbourhood.

Between the years 1865 and 1870 the Company were unable to meet the demands made upon them for water. In 1869 Sir John Hawkseley, in a report to the Company, stated that the supply was only 10 to 14 gallons per day per head of the population, and that the supply was quite inadequate to meet the requirements of the district. He stated that “the supply was quite insufficient for the wants of the district, and that there was a positive want of the first element of life.”

From that time until the present the supply has been a source of dissatisfaction to the inhabitants of the district. The Company have made no attempt to improve their sources of supply, and several schemes which have been brought forward have been abandoned. The Company have been unable to obtain a sufficient supply of water, and the district has suffered in consequence.
