## Page 09

Now between the northern limits of the Borough of Tynemouth and the River Blyth, (that is
in the district through which the gravitation main from the Font must pass) there is a population of
45,000 people without a reliable supply of water. Considerably over half of this population are
supplied by the Newcastle Company but only under a day-to-day agreement. Under one of its Acts of
Parliament the Newcastle Company is prohibited from entering into any agreement with any local
Water Authority (always excepting Tynemouth) to supply it with water without the consent of the
Newcastle Corporation, and this consent the Newcastle Corporation has in the past refused. This
day-to-day agreement means, therefore, that while the Newcastle Company supplies water to certain
localities to-day, it is not under obligation to supply, and for any or no reason whatever may not supply
water to these localities to-morrow.
If, theretore, these districts are afforded an opportunity of
obtaining a reliable supply of water under agreement with the Tynemouth Corporation, at the rate, say,
of sixpence per thousand gallons, which is the price now being paid by them for their most uncertain
supply from the Newcastle Company, it may be reasonably expected that the opportunity will be
embraced by them, and that the sale for a large portion of the surplus water which Tynemouth
would have at its disposal would be procured.

As a second alternative, therefore, Tynemouth has before it a bold scheme which involves an
expenditure of nearly half-a-million of money, with, on the one side, a possible immediate annual
deficit of f6,100, to which must be added the annual instalments of the amount to be paid for the
purchase of the North Shields Company's undertaking; and, on the other, a possible annual surplus,
which would be devoted towards the extinction of the debt incurred in acquiring the Company's under-
taking; and in either case, the ultimate acquisition of a valuable asset in the shape of a waterworks
free from debt.

Another aspect of the case must, however, be submitted.
Assuming for the moment that the
Council or the Ratepayers decide against the proposal to promote a Bill for obtaining water from the
Font, what then will be the position of Tynemouth ? Water, under such circumstances, could be
obtained only from the Newcastle Co., and Tynemouth would have to approach that Company as a
suppliant. It must be remembered that the Newcastle Co. is under no obligation whatever to supply
more than 440,000 gallons daily (and then that obligation is only temporary), while the present total
requirements are nearly 1,500,000 gallons. Fourpence half-penny per 1,000 gallons is the price now
asked by the Newcastle Co., but if the present opportunity for securing itself is missed by Tynemouth,
and it is compelled to approach the Newcastle Co., then it will be within the power of that Company
to drive a very hard bargain, and make Tynemouth pay to the uttermost farthing all that it can afford
for water. Such a burden it is impossible to estimate. That is the serious position in which Tyne-
mouth is likely to find itself if this Bill is not proceeded with.

On the contrary, what will be the position of Tynemouth if the Bill should be promoted ?
Negotiations with the Newcastle Co. may still proceed, and Tynemouth will then be in a position to
to bargain also, as it would have an alternative scheme to fall back upon. The Newcastle Co, if it is
anxious for the custom of Tynemouth, will then be prepared to supply water to this District upon the
cheapest terms possible to the Company, and Tynemouth, if such terms of supply should prove to be more
favourable than those it could otherwise obtain for itself by getting water from an independent source,
could accept the Company's terms and withdraw its Bill. If the Company's terms however were not
so favourable, then Tynemouth would persevere with its Bill. It is evident that Tynemouth may
therefore occupy amost advantageous position, and obtain, taking all things into consideration, a
water supply at the cheapest possible rate instead of probably at the dearest.
