In order to prevent settlement the walls above this caisson were cantilevered from the wall of the adjoining caisson which was firmly founded on the sandstone. The reinforcement used was steel rails—two in each wall, and no settlement has occurred. The side walls of the gallery were constructed in mass concrete nine inches thick spanned internally by arches at intervals of eleven feet six inches. The gallery is covered with a reinforced concrete slab six inches in thickness.

Water passes from the main gallery, through an opening eighteen inches square in the downstream wall, into an outlet well. The velocity is checked by a concrete baffle wall to create settlement of suspended matter to the dished floor of the outlet well. Connected with the outlet well is a sluice chamber controlled by a twelve-inch penstock, through which the water passes into the eight-inch diameter supply main. Cleaning of the outlet well is effected in the following manner: a four-inch diameter vent pipe is fitted in the well, the bottom of the pipe being six inches above the bottom of the dished floor and the top of the pipe carried up above maximum top water level. At a height of four feet six inches above the bottom of the vent pipe a four-inch diameter scour pipe is connected and carried out through the outlet well; this branch is laid downstream and controlled by a valve. To “scour out” the well, the twelve-inch penstock is closed thus permitting water to rise in the well to a height of five feet above the branch. The scour valve is then opened and the silt in the well gently stirred with a rod causing it to pass out through the scour pipe. Had it been possible to obtain a greater fall on the scour pipe and an increased head on the branch, the scouring would have been automatic in so far as it would not be necessary to promote agitation of the sediment by means of the rod. Such conditions, however, were not possible in this instance.

The total area of the main gallery is 350 square feet and the average rate of flow from the gallery, as measured by a “V” notch, is 175 gallons per minute an equivalent of 0.5 gallons per square foot per minute or thirty gallons per square foot per hour.

The rate of flow from this gallery was insufficient and it was necessary to augment the flow by constructing a second instalment of galleries higher up the grotto. The second instalment consists of three galleries three feet six inches deep and four feet wide (Fig. 5). These galleries are covered with a reinforced concrete slab and are constructed five feet six inches below the bed of the grotto. Downstream of each gallery, at a distance of fifty feet, a wall of dry rubble was built across the grotto to form a sand trap and to prevent the galleries from being scoured out by flood water. The concrete of the upstream walls of these galleries was not rammed too tightly, consequently they are porous and permit of seepage into the gallery. The upper galleries are connected with each other and with the main gallery by a six-inch diameter cast-iron pipe. (Fig. 1).
