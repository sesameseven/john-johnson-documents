DESCRIPTION OF THE WORKS.
The scheme is designed for a supply of 500,000 gallons per day. The works consist of an intake comprising four covered infiltration galleries, and from these the water passes through four miles of eight-inch diameter cast-iron supply main to a covered reservoir having a capacity of 930,000 gallons. From the reservoir water is distributed in the Township by twelve miles of cast-iron service mains varying in diameter from ten to three inches. As the water is collected in underground galleries at the intake, stored in a covered reservoir and naturally filtered at the source, it was considered that filter beds were not necessary.

CONSTRUCTION.
Construction of the first instalment of the intake works was commenced on August 1st, 1924, and was completed at the end of the same month, in spite of the fact that the work was hindered by heavy tornadoes which washed large quantities of sand into the excavation.

During the previous dry season, observations were taken of the sub-soil flow by pumping out trial holes of known areas, with a view to obtaining data on which to base calculations to find the area required in the main infiltration gallery. The information gained from these trial holes was unsatisfactory, due to the impossibility of preventing surface water from percolating behind the lining of the trial holes; the result obtained was two and a half gallons per square foot per minute through the porous sandstone forming the bed of the grotto.

A figure of one gallon per square foot per minute was therefore adopted when determining the size of the main gallery. This gallery was finally constructed eighty-four feet in length, four feet two inches in width and eight feet in depth. (Figs. 1, 2, 3.) It completely spans the grotto and abuts against the sandstone walls at a point where they converge close to the mouth of the grotto. After tornadoes there is a considerable stormwater “run off” from the gathering ground into the grotto and it was feared that unless this gallery was heavily constructed it might be washed away by the weight of flood water. It was decided therefore to construct it in the form of a “Skewback” arch in plan (Fig. 2). The gallery is founded on caissons having inside dimensions of ten feet by four feet two inches and three feet deep, with walls nine inches in thickness. The caissons were carried down to the solid sandstone with the exception of one at the north end of the gallery.
