Present Consumption (April 1928) is as follows:—
| | gallons per day|
| ---- | -----: |
| Domestic Supply | 130,000 |
| Railway | 80,000 |
| Colliery | 10,000 |
| | 220,000 | 

and costs (at this rate of consumption) 10d. per 1,000 gallons.

The available supply is estimated at 500,000 gallons per day, but it is improbable that the works will ever be able to operate at full output, unless further capital expenditure is incurred necessitating increased running costs.

Assuming an effective ultimate output of 440,000 gallons, after an additional capital expenditure of say £10,000 and assuming the present running costs would be double, the maintenance charges including interest and depreciation would be increased to £4,343 *per annum*.

This gives a rate of 6¾d. per 1,000 gallons. Consumption is continually increasing and it is probable therefore that the present figure of 10d. per 1,000 gallons will gradually fall to a minimum of 6¾d. per 1,000 gallons.

The actual annual receipts from seventy-six domestic services now amount to £202 10s. 0d. representing 13,300 gallons at 10d. per 1,000 gallons, this figure will be considerably increased next year when the Southern Secretariat Headquarters are transferred to Enugu. At the present time free supplies are provided to Police, Prisons, Hospitals, etc. Paper charges to the value of £327 *per annum* might be made on account of these services, representing 21,500 gallons at 10d. per 1,000 gallons.
